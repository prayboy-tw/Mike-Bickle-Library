
# Session #16 Persecution Unto the Fullness of All That God Ordained

>“The friend of the Bridegroom…rejoices greatly because of the Bridegroom’s voice.” (Jn. 3:29)

## I. PERSECUTION IS ALLOWED FOR THE BENEFIT OF GOD'S PEOPLE

A. Persecution is both a demonic attack to hinder our spiritual growth and a divine gift to enhance it.

B. God granted persecution (Phil. 1:29) that they may be transformed by it for their benefit in this age (1 Pet. 4:1, 14) and in the age to come (2 Cor. 4:17).

><sup>1</sup>…for he who has <ins>suffered</ins> in the flesh <ins>has ceased from sin</ins>, <sup>2</sup>that he <ins>no longer</ins> should live the rest of his time…for the <ins>lusts of men</ins>... <sup>14</sup>If you are reproached for the name of Christ, <ins>blessed</ins> are you, <ins>for the Spirit of glory</ins>…rests on you…[and] He is glorified. (1 Pet. 4:1-2, 14)

><sup>17</sup>Our light affliction…<ins>is working for us</ins> a far more exceeding…weight of glory… (2 Cor. 4:17)

## II. PERSECUTION AS PROOF OF GOD’S GOOD LEADERSHIP: 4 EVIDENCES

A. Paul spoke of persecution as evidence or proof of God’s righteous judgment or wise leadership (2 Thes. 1:3-11). Paul identified 4 ways that give evidence that persecution benefits God’s people.

><sup>3</sup>…<ins>your faith</ins> grows exceedingly, and <ins>the love</ins> of every one of you all abounds…<sup>4</sup>so that… [we boast of]…<ins>your patience</ins> [perseverance] and faith in all your persecutions…that you endure, <sup>5</sup>which is <ins>manifest evidence</ins> [proof] of the <ins>righteous judgment of God</ins> [good leadership], that you may be <ins>counted worthy</ins> of [prepared to experience more of] the kingdom…<sup>6</sup>since it is a righteous thing with God <ins>to repay with tribulation</ins> those who trouble you, <sup>7</sup>and to <ins>give you rest</ins>… when the Lord Jesus is revealed… (2 Thes. 1:3-7)

B. ***Evidence #1:*** The fact that they had ***patience*** or perseverance when persecuted is proof that God’s good leadership provided them with grace or supernatural ability to endure with faith and love.

C. ***Evidence #2:*** Persecution rightly responded to results in being ***“counted worthy”*** or equipped to experience more kingdom activity. “Worthy” doesn’t mean “deserving of,” but “suited for,” or “prepared for.” Persecution purifies us by leading us to align more with God’s leadership. Persecution is an effective “accelerant” to maturing in godliness which in turn increases our spiritual capacity to enjoy God and experience more kingdom activity in our lives.

><sup>35</sup>Some of those of understanding <ins>shall fall</ins> [martyred], to <ins>refine</ins> and <ins>purify</ins> them… (Dan. 11:35)

D. ***Evidence #3:*** God’s wisdom in ***repaying with tribulation*** those who persecute His people becomes evident as their evil deeds and love of darkness are manifest (Jn. 3:19).

E. ***Evidence #4:*** God gives them ***ultimate rest*** when they receive “all the good pleasure of His goodness” (2 Thes. 1:11) in the age to come related to their spiritual maturity resulting from persecution. Thus it helps us grow unto being worthy (prepared) to receive the fullness of our calling.

><sup>6</sup>…it is a righteous thing with God…<sup>7</sup>to <ins>give you rest</ins>…when the Lord Jesus is revealed… <sup>11</sup>We pray <ins>always</ins> for you that God would <ins>count you worthy</ins> of this calling, and <ins>fulfill all the good pleasure</ins> of His goodness…<sup>12</sup>that…Christ be glorified in you, and <ins>you in Him</ins>… (2 Thes. 1:6-12)

F. Oh, to be counted worthy of a full reward (2 Jn. 8) on Judgment Day without any loss (1 Cor. 3:15)!

><sup>14</sup>If anyone’s work which he has built on it endures, <ins>he will receive a reward</ins>. <sup>15</sup>If anyone’s work is burned, <ins>he will suffer loss</ins>; but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire. (1 Cor. 3:14-15)

><sup>8</sup>…that we <ins>do not lose</ins> those things we worked for, but that <ins>we may receive a full reward</ins>. (2 Jn. 8)

G. Paul wrote of his passion to obtain a heavenly crown around AD 54 (1 Cor. 9:24-27). He was zealous not to be disqualified from receiving “the prize” or an imperishable crown (9:25).

><sup>24</sup>…[I run to] receive <ins>the prize</ins>? Run in such a way that <ins>you may obtain</ins>… <sup>25</sup>an <ins>imperishable crown</ins>… <sup>27</sup>I discipline my body…lest…I myself should become <ins>disqualified</ins>. (1 Cor. 9:24-27)

><sup>11</sup>[Jesus said]…“Hold fast what you have, that <ins>no one may take your crown</ins>.” (Rev. 3:11)

H. Near the end of Paul’s life, he _pressed_ for _the prize_ of the upward or heavenly call (Phil. 3:14). He wrote this around AD 62 or 8 years after he wrote of “running to win the prize” (1 Cor. 9:24).

><sup>12</sup>Not that I have already attained…<ins>I press on</ins>, that I may <ins>lay hold of</ins> that for which Christ Jesus has also <ins>laid hold of me</ins> [fullness of his calling]… <sup>14</sup><ins>I press toward</ins> the goal for <ins>the prize</ins> of the upward call [the fullness of his heavenly calling] of God in Christ Jesus. (Phil. 3:12-14)

I. He lived for 6 more years, dying in AD 68 “with assurance” of obtaining a crown (2 Tim. 4:8).

><sup>7</sup>I have finished the race…<sup>8</sup><ins>there is laid up for me</ins> the <ins>crown of righteousness</ins>. (2 Tim. 4:7-8)

J. Peter declared that the maturing of our faith through persecution is more precious than gold.

><sup>6</sup>…by various trials, <sup>7</sup>that the <ins>genuineness [maturity] of your faith</ins>, being <ins>much more precious than gold</ins>…may be found to <ins>praise, honor, and glory</ins> at the revelation of Jesus… (1 Pet. 1:6-7)

K. Paul understood the benefits of persecution and longed to know the fellowship of suffering.

><sup>10</sup>That I may <ins>know Him</ins> and [His] power…and the <ins>fellowship of His sufferings</ins>… (Phil. 3:10)
