
# Session #32 Isaiah 40: Five Messages that Prepare the Way of the Lord

>“The friend of the Bridegroom…rejoices greatly because of the Bridegroom’s voice.” (Jn. 3:29)

## I. INTRODUCTION

A. Isaiah issued a twofold mandate to the Lord’s messengers — to comfort to Jerusalem (40:1-2; 62:11) and to prepare the way of the Lord or to “build a highway for God” (40:3-5). Then the Holy Spirit showed Isaiah 5 prophetic messages that the Lord's messengers were to emphasize (40:6-31).

B. ***Mandate #1***: The Lord’s messengers are mandated to speak a message of comfort to Jerusalem (40:1-2). There are two groups addressed in Isaiah 40:1. The Lord commands the global body of Christ as those who love and listen to God’s Word — yet who are distinct from Jerusalem — to give a message of comfort to a group referred to as “My people Jerusalem” — those living in Jerusalem. Jerusalem will receive a double portion of favor in the Millennium (61:7; cf. 60:1-22; 62:1-7).

><sup>1</sup>“Comfort, yes, <Ins>comfort My people</Ins>!” Says your God. <sup>2</sup>“<Ins>Speak comfort</Ins> to Jerusalem, and <Ins>cry out</Ins> to her, that her <Ins>warfare</Ins> is ended [when Jesus returns], that her iniquity is <Ins>pardoned</Ins>; for she has received from the LORD’s hand <Ins>double</Ins> for all her sins.” (Isa. 40:1-2)

C. ***Mandate #2***: The call to prepare the way of the Lord (isa. 40:3-5)

><sup>3</sup>The voice of one crying in the wilderness: “<Ins>Prepare the way of the LORD</Ins>; make straight in the desert a <Ins>highway for our God</Ins>. <sup>4</sup>Every valley shall be exalted and every mountain and hill brought low; the crooked places shall be made straight and the rough places smooth; <sup>5</sup>the <Ins>glory of the LORD</Ins> shall be revealed, and <Ins>all flesh shall see it together</Ins>…” (Isa. 40:3-5)

D. ***All flesh shall see it together***: Isaiah anchored this “messenger mandate” in an end-time context when all nations see the glory of God (40:5). Thus, the primary context here is on preparing people spiritually for Jesus’ second coming when all the nations will see His glory (40:5, 10). In John the Baptist’s ministry, *not everyone saw God’s glory*. His ministry was a partial fulfillment of 40:3-5.

><sup>7</sup>Behold, He is coming with clouds, and <Ins>every eye will see Him</Ins>…<Ins>all the tribes</Ins> of the earth… (Rev. 1:7)

E. ***Highway for God***: It’s a highway that serves God’s purposes by preparing people to walk with God spiritually (before Jesus returns) and to come to Him in Jerusalem physically (after Jesus returns).

1. ***A spiritual highway***: A basic kingdom principle is that God releases His power through people who walk in agreement with His values and plans. The forerunner message prepares His way — a *highway of purity* (God’s values) and a *highway of understanding* (God’s plans).

><sup>3</sup>Can two <Ins>walk together</Ins>, unless they are <Ins>agreed</Ins>? (Amos 3:3)

2. John called people to repent and live in agreement with God’s values and plans (Lk. 3:8-17).

><sup>8</sup>…<Ins>bear fruits worthy of repentance</Ins>… <sup>10</sup>The people asked…, “<Ins>What shall we do then</Ins>?” <sup>11</sup>He answered…, “He who has two tunics, <Ins>let him give to him who has none</Ins>…” <sup>16</sup>One mightier than I <Ins>is coming</Ins>…He will baptize you with the <Ins>Holy Spirit</Ins> and fire. (Lk. 3:8-16)

## II. FIVE MESSAGES THAT PREPARE PEOPLE FOR THE COMING OF LORD (ISA. 40:6-31)

A. Isaiah asked for the specific message that will comfort Jerusalem and prepare a highway for God. The Lord answered, saying that “all flesh is grass but God’s promises stand forever” (40:6-8).
***Message #1***: To prioritize cultivating an eternal perspective of the kingdom (2 Cor. 4:17-18)

><sup>6</sup>The voice said, “<Ins>Cry out</Ins>!” And he said, “<Ins>What shall I cry</Ins>?” “<Ins>All flesh is grass</Ins>, and all its loveliness is like the flower of the field…<sup>8</sup>[it] withers…but the <Ins>word of our God stands forever</Ins>.” (Isa. 40:6-8)

><sup>17</sup>For our light affliction….<Ins>is working for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory</Ins>, <sup>18</sup>while <Ins>we do not look at the things</Ins> which are seen, <Ins>but at the things which are not seen</Ins>. For the things…seen are <Ins>temporary</Ins>, but the things which are not seen are eternal. (2 Cor. 4:17-18)

B. ***Message #2***: To proclaim Jesus coming as a strong Warrior and tender Shepherd (Isa. 40:9-11). Jerusalem in the end times will be surrounded by the armies of the Antichrist (Zech. 12:2; 14:2).

><sup>9</sup>…<Ins>say</Ins> to the cities of Judah, “Behold your <Ins>God shall come</Ins> with a <Ins>strong hand</Ins>… <sup>11</sup>He will feed His flock <Ins>like a shepherd</Ins>; He will <Ins>gather</Ins> the lambs…<Ins>carry them</Ins>…and <Ins>gently lead</Ins> them…” (Isa. 40:9-11)

C. ***Message #3***: To proclaim that their coming Messiah has all power and wisdom (Isa. 40:12-20). He understands everything, fulfills all of His promises, and perfectly administrates justice (40:14).

><sup>12</sup>Who has <Ins>measured the waters</Ins> in the hollow of His hand, <Ins>measured heaven</Ins> with a span and <Ins>calculated the dust</Ins> of the earth…<Ins>Weighed the mountains</Ins> in scales…? <sup>15</sup>The <Ins>nations are as a drop in a bucket</Ins>, and are counted as the <Ins>small dust</Ins> on the scales…<sup>17</sup><Ins>All nations</Ins> [Antichrist’s empire] before Him are as nothing…<sup>18</sup><Ins>To whom then will you liken God</Ins>? (Isa. 40:12-18)

D. ***Message #4***: To proclaim that Jesus easily prevails over all oppressive nations (Isa. 40:21-26).

><sup>22</sup>It is <Ins>He who sits above the circle of the earth</Ins>, and its inhabitants are like <Ins>grasshoppers</Ins>… <sup>23</sup>He brings the <Ins>princes to nothing</Ins>; He makes the <Ins>judges</Ins> of the earth useless. <sup>24</sup>Scarcely shall they be <Ins>planted</Ins>…scarcely shall [they] <Ins>take root</Ins>…<Ins>He will blow on them</Ins>, and they will wither… <sup>25</sup>“To whom then will you <Ins>liken Me</Ins>?”…<sup>26</sup><Ins>Lift up your eyes</Ins>…see who has created these things, who brings out their <Ins>host by number</Ins>; <Ins>He calls them all by name</Ins>… (Isa. 40:22-26)

E. ***Message #5***: To proclaim how to respond to the Lord and how to be strengthened (Isa. 40:27-31). Israel’s complaint was that their life was “hidden” from God who overlooked them in their crisis.

><sup>27</sup>Why do you say, O Jacob… “<Ins>My way is hidden</Ins> from the LORD, and <Ins>my just claim is passed over</Ins> by my God”? <sup>28</sup>Have you not known?…The everlasting God…<Ins>neither faints nor is weary</Ins>. <Ins>His understanding is unsearchable</Ins>. <sup>29</sup><Ins>He gives power to the weak</Ins>… <sup>30</sup>Even the <Ins>youths shall faint and be weary</Ins>, and the young men shall <Ins>utterly fall</Ins>, <sup>31</sup>but those who <Ins>wait on the LORD</Ins> shall <Ins>renew their strength</Ins>; they shall mount up with wings like eagles…and not be weary…” (Isa. 40:27-31)

F. To wait on God is to engage with His heart and plans — we cultivate intimacy with God by spending time prayer-reading His Word, serving Him, loving people, etc. Waiting on God is not about “just biding time,” but actively engaging with God's heart and contending for His promises.
